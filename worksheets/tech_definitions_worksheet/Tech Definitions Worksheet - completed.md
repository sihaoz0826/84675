# Tech Definitions Worksheet — completed

Coding rule: mark a column only when the **wording on the worksheet** clearly includes that idea. Do not infer from the author’s broader work.

## What each column means

| Column | Mark when the definition… |
|---|---|
| Practical Ability | Treats technology as a practical means, skill, ability, or application (not only as objects or abstract knowledge). |
| Human Creativity | Names ingenuity, creativity, invention, or design. |
| Effort and Intention | Names effort, attempt, pursuit, purpose, plan, or other directed human striving. |
| Organized Knowledge | Names knowledge, science, information, or rational/systematic organization. |
| Solution-Oriented | Names problems or problem-solving. |
| Tangible | Requires or includes tools, artifacts, machines, instruments, or the material world. |
| Includes Practices | Includes skills, methods, processes, systems, or ways of doing — not objects alone. |
| Modifies the Natural | Claims technology acts on nature, the world, or the material/physical environment. |
| Improves | Claims technology makes things better, more efficient, or advantageous. |

## Why each row is marked that way

**Marc Andreessen** — “lever for being able to” (Practical Ability); “take natural resources” (Modifies the Natural); “make something better” (Improves).

**Read Bain** — lists tools/machines/etc. (Tangible) **and** “the skills by which we produce and use them” (Practical Ability + Includes Practices). No claim about knowledge, nature, or improvement.

**Ian Barbour** — “application of organized knowledge to practical tasks” (Practical Ability, Effort and Intention, Organized Knowledge) “by ordered systems of people and machines” (Includes Practices, Tangible). Practical tasks ≠ problem-solving.

**C.L. Bush** — Broadest definition on the sheet. Science/mechanics (Organized Knowledge) applied “to the solution of problems” (Solution-Oriented); resources/tools (Tangible); processes/personnel/systems (Includes Practices); “perform tasks” (Practical Ability); “developed to… create… advantages” (Effort and Intention + Improves). “Ecological… context” is the setting, not a claim that tech remakes nature.

**Martin van Creveld** — “abstract system of knowledge” (Organized Knowledge); “method for solving its problems” (Solution-Oriented + Includes Practices). Abstract, so not Tangible.

**Jacques Ellul** — “methods rationally arrived at” (Includes Practices + Effort and Intention) “having absolute efficiency” (Improves). He is defining *technique*, not gadgets.

**Frederick Ferré** — “Practical implementations of intelligence” (Practical Ability + Effort and Intention), “concretely embodied in artefacts or practices” (Tangible + Includes Practices). Intelligence ≠ organized knowledge; practical ≠ better.

**John Fernald** — Only “ability to convert… resources into output” (Practical Ability). Dry production-function definition; no tools, knowledge, or improvement language.

**Robert Friedel #1** — “knowledge and instruments” used “to accomplish the purposes of life” (Organized Knowledge, Tangible, Practical Ability, Effort and Intention). He excludes purely mental processes, so practices are not included.

**Robert Friedel #2** — “pursuit of power over nature” (Effort and Intention + Modifies the Natural). Nothing else.

**John Kenneth Galbraith** — Almost Mesthene: “systematic application of scientific or other organized knowledge to practical tasks” (Organized Knowledge, Practical Ability, Effort and Intention). No artifacts or practices named.

**Thomas P. Hughes #1** — “creativity process involving human ingenuity” (Human Creativity + Includes Practices). The distinctive claim is ingenuity, not effort.

**Thomas P. Hughes #2** — “effort” (Effort and Intention) “to organize the world” (Modifies the Natural) “for problem solving” (Solution-Oriented) so goods/services can be “invented, developed, produced, and used” (Human Creativity + Includes Practices).

**Thomas P. Hughes #3** — “Problem-solving systems” (Solution-Oriented + Includes Practices) “concerned with the reordering of the material world” (Effort and Intention, Tangible, Modifies the Natural).

**David M. Kaplan** — “technique and activities” plus “implements and artifacts” inside social organization where they are “developed, employed, and administered” (Includes Practices, Tangible, Effort and Intention). Worksheet excerpt does not mention nature or knowledge.

**Immanuel Kant** — “rational and ordered attempt” (Organized Knowledge + Effort and Intention) “to control his natural environment” (Modifies the Natural).

**Kranzberg & Purcell** — “much more than tools and artifacts… processes” (Tangible + Includes Practices); “man’s attempts to satisfy his wants by human action on physical objects” (Effort and Intention + Modifies the Natural).

**Edwin Mansfield** — “society’s pool of knowledge” plus “day-to-day operations of production” (Organized Knowledge, Practical Ability, Includes Practices). Knowledge about industry, not objects.

**J.B. McOmber** — Narrowest: “simply its status as a tool” (Practical Ability as instrumentality + Tangible). Explicitly nothing else.

**Emmanuel Mesthene** — “organization of knowledge for the achievement of practical purposes” (Organized Knowledge, Effort and Intention, Practical Ability). Thierer’s favorite; knowledge-for-purpose, no artifacts.

**Joel Mokyr** — Defines *technological progress*: “application of information to the production process” (Organized Knowledge + Includes Practices) “to increase efficiency” / “better or new products” (Improves).

**Paul Nightingale** — “entities produced by a problem solving process” (Tangible, Solution-Oriented, Includes Practices) that “changes and transforms the world” (Modifies the Natural) to match a “plan, or design” (Human Creativity + Effort and Intention).

**Richard Rhodes** — “application of science, engineering and industrial organization” (Organized Knowledge, Effort and Intention, Includes Practices) “to create a human-built world” (Tangible + Modifies the Natural).

**Charles Singer** — “How things are commonly done or made” (Includes Practices + Practical Ability) “and what things are done or made” (Tangible). Descriptive, not normative.

**Bernard Stiegler** — “pursuit of life by means other than life” (Effort and Intention, Practical Ability as means, Includes Practices, Modifies the Natural as beyond biological life).

**Peter Thiel** — “any new and better way of doing things” (Includes Practices, Practical Ability, Improves). No tools or knowledge required.

**Christian Wolff** — “science of skills and works of skill” / “things made by man’s labour… hands” (Organized Knowledge, Practical Ability, Includes Practices, Effort and Intention, Tangible).

## Close calls (change these if class discussion goes another way)

- **Bush → Improves:** “advantages” is not the word “better,” but it is a gain claim. If the column is reserved for explicit better/efficiency language, uncheck it.
- **Hughes #2 → Human Creativity:** coded because of “invented.” If the column requires the words creativity/ingenuity, uncheck it.
- **Stiegler → Modifies the Natural:** “means other than life” is about exceeding biology, not dominating nature. If the column needs nature/world language, uncheck it.
- **Kranzberg → Modifies the Natural:** “physical objects” is material, not “nature.” Same issue.
- **Mansfield → Practical Ability:** industrial-arts know-how is practical, but the headline word is “knowledge.” If Practical Ability is reserved for “ability/skill/means” wording, uncheck it.
- **Barbour / Galbraith / Mesthene → Practical Ability:** coded because of “practical tasks/purposes.” If that column means *ability* only (Fernald/Andreessen), uncheck those three.

## Pattern to notice

Most definitions agree technology is **practical and practice-like**. They split on whether it must be **knowledge**, must be **physical**, must **solve problems**, must **remake nature**, and must **make things better**. McOmber is the tool-only pole; Bush and Nightingale are the kitchen-sink pole; Ellul, van Creveld, and Friedel #2 treat technology as a stance or method rather than a gadget.
