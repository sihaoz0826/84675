# On Technological Determinism — Allan Dafoe

**What this is:** A 2015 theory paper in *Science, Technology, & Human Values*. Dafoe wants scholars to stop using “technological determinism” as an insult and treat it as a real idea again.

**Thesis:** Technology opens a door; war and market competition drag us through. Up close, people have lots of choice. Over long stretches of history, they often do not.

**Technological determinism** (plain English): the idea that tech drives history, and that society has to follow tech. Soft version: new tools have a life of their own and push how we live. Hard version: we cannot really steer it.

## Main points

- After the 1980s, technology scholars dumped this idea and treated it as a straw man. Dafoe says reclaim it. The question is not “right or wrong?” but how much, in what ways, and when.
- “Technology” is not just gadgets. It is anything that *works* — tools, habits, rules, whole systems. The key is function, not metal.
- Determinist claims come in a family: tools shape us; tech seems to follow its own path; we sleepwalk, or take a “magnificent bribe” (comfort in exchange for going along).
- Tools can carry politics (speed bumps, surveillance). Old systems get stuck because so much is already built and paid for. Side effects no one planned can lock us in. If those effects are strong and surprises are common, even mainstream work slides toward the hard version.
- Over long time, tech looks like it trends: faster, deadlier, more complex. Some of that is just “you need the simple thing first.” Some looks almost automatic (chips getting denser). Some is people not noticing, or being paid to cheer.
- Close-up studies find choice and mess. Big-picture studies find force and pattern. Both can be true — like traffic: each driver chooses, but jams still happen. Do not kill the big picture with a shop-floor story.
- The missing “why”: if groups compete hard enough for money or military power, and new tech lets some win, winning ways of living spread. Others copy or get crushed. If some societies chase powerful tech, the rest get dragged along.
- People often adapt in advance. That can look like “our values,” but the real pressure is survive or lose. Britain grew its power grid against its politics in World War I. Japan dropped isolation after Perry’s ships: adapt or be dominated.
- This is not the only path, and it is weaker when war is less of a threat. The most locked-in cases are wars between strangers. The most steerable cases are markets we can write rules for. If we want more control, we have to regulate the competition.
- Hard determinism says change is inevitable (gives up). Extreme “we construct everything” ignores the squeeze of competition (also gives up). Ask how much agency, where, and why.

## Lines worth remembering

- Technology opens the door. War and market competition drag us through.
- Wrong question: is determinism true? Right questions: how much, how, and when.
- Up close we choose; over long history, competition often chooses for us.
- Adapt or be dominated.
- Want to steer history? Understand — and regulate — the kinds of competition we live under.
