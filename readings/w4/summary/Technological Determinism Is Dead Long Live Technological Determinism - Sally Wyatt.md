# Technological Determinism Is Dead; Long Live Technological Determinism — Fast Read

**Sally Wyatt** | Chapter 7 in *The Handbook of Science and Technology Studies*, 3rd ed. (MIT Press, 2008), 165–180.

**What this is:** An STS handbook chapter. Wyatt says the idea that “tech drives history” is not dead.

**Thesis:** Do not ignore technological determinism and hope it goes away. Take it seriously, split it into types, and notice the technological determinists in ourselves.

- It is still everywhere: in what people do and how they justify it, in how analysts and politicians talk, and in everyday reactions to new machines.
- We already name ages and countries by their tools (stone, steam, computer; Holland and windmills; the U.S. and cars). That habit feels like common sense.
- The idea has two parts, both needed: tech grows on its own, outside society; and tech change then drives social change. People also treat tech progress as social progress (cheerleaders and pessimists both do this).
- It lasts because it is simple and matches daily life. Most of us did not design our tools and just hope they keep working.
- The political problem: it leaves no room for choice. It lets us — and the people who build the stuff — dodge responsibility for the machines we make and use.
- STS should not just write fancier stories and wait. Treat this idea as something real people use, not as a dumb myth.
- Treat actors’ ideas the same as scholars’ ideas — including this one. If you only “follow the actors,” you can miss users. If you refuse to follow them when they say tech drives change, you miss why that story works.
- Other maps exist (hard vs soft; start with the machines on hand; society shaped by tech *in use*; big systems gaining “momentum”). Wyatt’s four types are the ones to keep.

**Her four types, in plain words**

- **Justificatory:** people use “the tech made us do it” to sell change — layoffs, policy, “the internet will automatically make life better.”
- **Descriptive:** scholars spot that story in others, call it a weak explanation, and write richer histories instead. (One economist: sometimes it actually fits the record.)
- **Methodological:** start your analysis with the machines a society has. STS’s guilty secret: if tech did not matter, the field would have nothing to study.
- **Normative:** tech has grown so big it slips political control. Winner’s charge: some STS work stopped asking who is accountable.

- She maps the types not because they are a good theory of society, but because lots of other people think they are.
- STS already uses the last two types. The new job is to take the first one seriously — the sales pitch.
- Keep wrestling with both directions: society shapes tech, and tech shapes society. The point is a more democratic say over the machines we live with.

## Lines worth remembering

- Technological determinism is dead; long live technological determinism.
- Two parts: tech grows outside society, and tech change drives social change.
- The idea lets us dodge responsibility for the technologies we make and use.
- Four types: justificatory, descriptive, methodological, normative.
- STS’s guilty secret: we are all technological determinists — or we would have nothing to study.
- Study the explanations, not just the people and the things.
