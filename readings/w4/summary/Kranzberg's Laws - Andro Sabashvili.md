# Kranzberg’s Laws

**What this is:** Andro Sabashvili’s recap of Melvin Kranzberg’s 1986 essay *Technology and History: Kranzberg’s Laws*.

**Thesis:** Six plain rules: technology is not morally simple; inventions create new needs; technologies come as systems; policy is decided by non-technical factors; the history of technology is the most useful history; and technology is a human activity that also remakes people.

- **First law:** Technology is neither good nor bad, and it is not neutral. The same tool can help or hurt depending on place, time, and scale. Effects spill far past the inventor’s purpose.
- “Good” tech used at huge scale often brings surprise harms. Nuclear power can give cheap energy or destroy the world. Weigh short-term vs long-term, hopes vs what actually happens, and trade-offs.
- **Second law:** Invention is the mother of necessity. A new device usually needs more inventions before it really works. Bell’s telephone needed a better microphone and switching systems.
- **Third law:** Technology comes in packages, big and small. Machines are systems of parts that depend on each other. Change one part and the rest must change. Do not study the parts alone.
- **Fourth law:** Even when tech sits at the center of a public fight, politics and social fears decide policy. Chicago built efficient power networks for profit; Berlin and London lagged because city governments protected their own power.
- After nuclear accidents, the public feared reactors more than cars, which kill far more people. Wanting a zero-risk world is itself the biggest risk.
- **Fifth law:** All history matters, but the history of technology matters most. Students find history useless because it is taught without tools, even though tools have always shaped daily life and the arts. Leonardo and Michelangelo were also engineers.
- **Sixth law:** Technology is a very human activity, and so is its history. People make tools; tools change people. A violin makes no music without the player, and the player needs the violin.

## Lines worth remembering

- Technology is neither good nor bad, nor is it neutral.
- Invention is the mother of necessity.
- Technology comes in packages; change one part and the rest must change.
- Non-technical factors decide tech policy. Seeking zero risk is itself the biggest risk.
- The history of technology is the most relevant history.
- People make technology, and technology remakes people.
