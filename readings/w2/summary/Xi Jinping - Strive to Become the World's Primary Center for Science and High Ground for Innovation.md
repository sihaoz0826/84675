# What this is

DigiChina (Stanford) translation of a May 28, 2018 Xi Jinping speech, published in *Qiushi* in March 2021 as China released the 14th Five-Year Plan (2021–26).

Webster’s intro point: the Plan’s S&T emphasis is not new. Publishing this 2018 speech tells Party readers the Plan continues work already underway. Context: Trump-era US pressure on Chinese access to key components; Xi pushing “independent/indigenous innovation” (自主创新) in “core technologies.” Month before, Xi called core technologies “important instruments of the state” and invoked “self-reliance.” He has also stressed semiconductors, quantum, AI. AI is #1 on the new Plan’s strategic list. The speech shows how Xi frames tech strategy to internal audiences, and signals continuity.

Speech venue: 19th CAS Academician Conference + 14th CAE Academician Conference. This is only a portion of the speech.

---

# Xi’s core claim

If China is to be strong, prosperous, and rejuvenated, it must develop S&T in a big way and become the world’s main science center and high ground of innovation. China is closer than ever to national rejuvenation, and therefore needs more than ever to become a world S&T superpower (世界科技强国).

This is a historical crossroads: the global S&T/industrial revolution meets China’s shift in development model. Unique opportunity + severe risk of falling further behind. Some crossroads come again; some are fleeting. Circumstances, challenge, and mission are all pressing. S&T workers must seize the moment, aim at the global frontier, and be pacesetters of new-era innovation.

---

# Diagnosis: what is wrong with China’s S&T

Vs. 19th Party Congress requirements, China is deficient in vision, innovation capacity, resource allocation, and institutions/policies.

- Basic research is weak; industry neglects it; China lacks major original achievements
- Weak basic industrial tech and processes
- Bottlenecks still prominent: industrial machine tools, high-end chips, basic software/hardware, development platforms, basic algorithms, basic components, basic materials
- Key/core technologies still controlled by others — this has not fundamentally changed
- R&D does not focus enough on industrial bottlenecks and demand
- Not enough globally minded open S&T cooperation
- Weak conversion of research into applications
- Talent systems and incentives are weak; shortage of top individuals and teams
- S&T administration is not fit for building a world S&T superpower
- Reform decisions have not formed strong cooperation
- S&T policy is poorly linked to economic/industrial policy
- Society still lacks an environment that encourages and tolerates innovation

# Global setting Xi describes

21st-century S&T is unprecedentedly intense. A new S&T/industrial revolution is remaking the global innovation map and economic structure.

Breakthrough areas:
- IT: AI, quantum information, mobile telecom, IoT, blockchain
- Life sciences: synthetic biology, gene editing, brain science, regenerative medicine
- Manufacturing: robotics + digitalization + new materials → intelligent, service-oriented, green manufacturing
- Energy: clean, efficient, sustainable → global energy revolution
- Space and maritime: expanding where humans can live and work

Overall: original breakthroughs in IT, life, manufacturing, energy, space, maritime feed cutting-edge and disruptive tech. Disciplines, science/tech, technologies, and natural vs. social sciences are fusing. S&T now shapes national fate and people’s welfare more than ever.

# Five tasks

## 1. Innovation is the first driving force → high-quality S&T supply for a modern economy

Innovation = the impetus that makes the economy move. Focus on quality and efficiency; support supply-side structural reform; raise the quality of the supply system. Drive three transformations of development: quality, efficiency, impetus. Make up shortcomings, tap potential, increase advantages. Reengineer industrial supply chains and upgrade value chains. Match supply with effective and potential demand. Boost confidence in the real economy.

The world is entering an information-industry-dominated era. Use digitalization, networking, and intelligent systems as levers for new growth. Cultivate strategic emerging industrial clusters as new pillars. Deeply fuse Internet, big data, and AI with the real economy to enlarge the digital economy. Make intelligent manufacturing the main direction of industrial upgrading. Use the new to drive out the old; use incremental change to upgrade the existing stock. Push Chinese industry toward the high end of global value chains.

## 2. Independent innovation, self-reliance, own the core

Self-reliance is the foundation of China’s standing in the world. Independent innovation is the only path up the world’s tech peaks. Innovation fails most of the time (9 of 10); still, persist without regret. Be confident, not self-deprecating or arrogant. Seize the commanding heights of tech competition.

Key slogan: core technologies cannot be asked for, bought, or begged. Only owning key/core technologies can guarantee economic security, defense security, and other securities.

Use the “four self-confidences” (path, theory, system, culture). Breakthrough points: key common technologies, frontier leading technologies, modern engineering technologies, disruptive innovation. Dare to take untrodden roads. Achieve independent control of key/core tech. Hold the initiative in innovation and development in China’s own hands.

A world S&T superpower needs iconic achievements. Strengthen strategy and goals; build first-mover advantages; concentrate elite forces on bottleneck (“chokepoint”) sectors; get breakthroughs fast. Strategic shift: from following → running alongside → leading. Be a leader in important domains and a pioneer in emerging interdisciplinary frontiers. Innovation should serve people’s livelihood: favor, benefit, and enrich the people.

Basic research is the source of the whole scientific system. Aim at global frontiers, play the “first move,” sit on the cold bench, plant trees and dig wells: long-term original work. Also intensify applied basic research. Use major S&T projects to open the “last kilometer,” tear down fences blocking industrialization, and link the innovation chain to the industrial chain. Turn samples → products → commodities.

Engineering/S&T are engines of progress. Workers need artisan spirit + unity. Aim at national strategy, economic construction, national security, livelihood, and military-civilian integration. Apply independent innovation in forward-looking, strategic areas.

## 3. Deepen S&T system reform; raise innovation-system efficiency

Innovation wins the future; reform is vital to the nation’s fortunes. S&T is the domain most in need of continuous reform.

2014 (two academies): the most urgent task for indigenous innovation is breaking system/mechanism barriers so S&T as primary productive force can be unleashed. Since then, the main reform architecture has been established.

2015 Implementation Plan: 143 reform tasks by 2020; 110+ done. Some long-unsolved problems have been cracked. Remaining problems:
- National innovation system still inefficient overall
- Resources still scattered, duplicated, low-efficiency
- “Many projects, many hats, many plaques”
- Low input–output efficiency
- Weak transfer, industrialization, market value
- Lagging reform of research institutes, S&T–finance integration, innovative talent training
- Researchers’ original-innovation drive still not fully unleashed

2018 = 40th anniversary of reform and opening. Do not waver. Crack hard nuts. “Poverty leads to change, change to development, development to longevity.”

Method: dual drivers of S&T innovation + institutional innovation. Problem-guided, demand-led. Work on actors, bases, resources, environments. Strengthen national strategic S&T forces. Clarify roles of enterprises, universities, research institutes along the innovation chain. Change government S&T management functions.

Enterprises are the subjects of innovation (Engels: social technical need drives science more than ten universities). Make firms the main actors in tech decisions, R&D input, research organization, and converting results. Grow vanguard firms with strong core and integrated innovation. Let the market decide R&D direction, pathways, factor prices, and allocation of innovation resources. Build long-term policy, input, incentive, service, and supervision mechanisms. Break “one dragon” bottlenecks from tech breakthrough → manufacturing → market → industry.

Also: high-standard national labs; coordinate large science plans, projects, centers, and international S&T bases; S&T advice for government decisions; think tanks; civil-military fusion (clear obstacles to “people joining the military” and “military transferring to the people”); stronger IP protection and services.

Funding/evaluation reform (from 2016): funding should serve people’s creative work, not the reverse. Categorized evaluation by quality, contribution, performance. Judge scientific, technical, economic, social, and cultural value. Several funding, knowledge-value pay, talent-evaluation, and award reforms exist but are incomplete; departments must finish implementing them and free scientists from bad funding and evaluation rules.

## 4. Global S&T governance; open independent innovation; China’s role in rules

S&T is global. Independent innovation is open-environment innovation — not closed-door. Draw on worldwide resources. Deepen international exchange; use global innovation resources; build win-win partnerships. Jointly face food security, energy security, human health, climate change, and future development. Benefit others while developing China; promote globally balanced development. This is framed as building a community of common destiny for humankind.

Do: plan innovation with a global vision; integrate into global innovation networks; open national S&T plans more; participate in and lead large international science plans; let Chinese scientists initiate international cooperation. Turn Belt and Road into an innovation road: S&T alliances and bases for corridor countries. Maximize use of global innovation resources. Raise China’s position in the global innovation landscape and its influence and rule-making power in global S&T governance.

## 5. Talent is the foundation

All innovation is done by people. Hard power and soft power ultimately rest on talent power. Whoever has first-class innovators and scientists gains the S&T advantage.

Problems: shortage of high-level and leading S&T talent; irrational evaluation (papers, titles, degrees); too many assessments; talent “hats” everywhere; management unfit for innovation.

Fixes: evaluate by innovation ability, quality, contribution; combine individual and team evaluation; recognize all participants’ real contribution; improve rewards so outstanding talent gets reasonable returns. Stop static “permanent card” labels. Stop using paper/patent/funding counts as the main standard. Do not let red tape and endless reports/approvals bind scientists.

Need four mechanisms: training, using talent fully, incentives for competing/growing, and competition so all kinds of talent emerge. Respect talent growth laws; fix structural imbalances; build a full echelon: international-level strategic S&T talent, leading talent, young talent, and innovation teams. Invest more; optimize policy; attract and employ talent from everywhere so they assemble in China and compete freely.
