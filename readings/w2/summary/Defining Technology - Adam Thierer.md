# Defining “Technology” — Fast Read (all main points)

**Author:** Adam Thierer | **Updated:** Oct 12, 2024
**What this is:** A living compendium of definitions of “technology,” listed by author, mostly modern. Not chronological (could later go back to Greek *techne*). Term only gained traction relatively recently.

---

## Thierer’s argument (the point of the piece)

1. Many books/essays on tech history and criticism **never define “technology.”**
2. That’s a problem: if you study or critique a technology/practice/development, you should say **how broadly or narrowly** you mean “technology” or “technological process.”
3. This list exists as a **resource**; he will keep adding; he wants suggestions.

**His favorite:** Mesthene — *organization of knowledge for practical purposes.*

**Recurring Thierer comments:**
- Hughes is right that tech is messy, contradictory, hard to define (like politics).
- Rhodes is right that even experts can’t cleanly separate science from technology.
- Kaplan’s long definition is “bloat” because modern tech is complex and we now see how much what we make matters (quoting Sacasas).
- Economists define it more dryly, and surprisingly few of them define it at all.

## The definitions (everything he collects)

**Merriam-Webster**
1. Practical application of knowledge (esp. in a field); a capability that application gives you.
2. A way of doing a task using technical processes/methods/knowledge.
3. Specialized aspects of a field.

**Oxford**
1. Application of scientific knowledge for practical purposes, esp. industry.
2. Machinery/devices from scientific knowledge.
3. The branch of knowledge = engineering / applied sciences.

**Emmanuel Mesthene** (1970, *Technological Change*) — Thierer’s favorite
> Organization of knowledge for the achievement of practical purposes.

**John Kenneth Galbraith** (1967, *The New Industrial State*) — similar to Mesthene
> Systematic application of scientific or other organized knowledge to practical tasks.

**Thomas P. Hughes**
- Opening (*Human-Built World*, 2004): tech is messy, complex, contradictory, full of folly, occasional good, rich in unintended consequences; defining it is as hard as defining politics.
- Then: **“a creativity process involving human ingenuity.”**
- Different book (*American Genesis*): **effort to organize the world for problem solving** so goods/services can be invented, developed, produced, and used.

**Martin van Creveld** (1989, *Technology and War*)
> An abstract system of knowledge, an attitude towards life, and a method for solving its problems.

**W. Brian Arthur** (2009, *The Nature of Technology*) — three meanings
1. A **means** to fulfill a human purpose (method, process, or device; simple or complex; material or not).
2. An **assemblage** of practices and components (plural).
3. The **entire collection** of devices and engineering practices available to a culture.

**Alfred P. Sloan Foundation / Richard Rhodes** (*Visions of Technology*, 1999)
- Sloan preface: **application of science, engineering, and industrial organization to create a human-built world.**
- Rhodes: that’s too simple. People say machines/engineering/science; **nobody (including experts) is sure where science ends and technology begins.**

**Joel Mokyr** (1990, *Lever of Riches*) — defines *technological progress*, not “technology”
> Any change in applying information to production that raises efficiency: same output with fewer resources, **or** better/new products.

**Edwin Mansfield** (1968/71, *Technological Change*)
- **Technology** = society’s pool of knowledge of the industrial arts: principles of physical/social phenomena + how to apply them to production + day-to-day production know-how.
- **Technological change** = advance of that pool: new methods for existing products, new designs with new characteristics, new org/marketing/management techniques.

**Read Bain** (1937, *American Sociological Review*)
> All tools, machines, utensils, weapons, instruments, housing, clothing, communicating and transporting devices **and the skills to produce and use them.**

**David M. Kaplan** (2003) — long / “beefy”
Technologies = systems combining technique + activities + implements/artifacts, inside social organizations that develop/employ/administer them. They change human activity and institutions by making worlds that shape culture and environment. Tech includes not just tools but **networks of social relations**; humanity and technology **co-shape** each other.

**Jacques Ellul** (1954/1964, *The Technological Society*) — *technique* vs *technology*
- **Technique** ≠ machines, technology, or one procedure.
- Technique = **totality of methods rationally arrived at and having absolute efficiency**, in **every** field of human activity.
- Not an isolated social fact; related to every factor in modern life; **a sociological phenomenon.**

**Bernard Stiegler** (1998, *Technics and Time, 1*)
> The pursuit of life by means other than life.

**Peter Thiel** (2014, *Zero to One*)
> Any new and better way of doing things is technology.

**Marc Andreessen** (2020 interview)
> The lever for taking natural resources and making something better out of them.

**Frederick Ferré** (1988, *Philosophy of Technology*) — concise + four limits
> Technologies = **practical implementations of intelligence.**
- Practical = not wholly ends in themselves (survival, health, comfort, material well-being).
- Implementations = concretely embodied (usually artifacts; sometimes social organization).

Four stipulations (to avoid over-breadth):
1. **Implemented, not empty-handed** — naked unmediated body = non-technological.
2. **Practical, not for its own sake.**
3. **Embodied, not only in the head** — don’t swallow pure mental methods (language, math) into “technology.”
4. **Intelligent, not blind** — not accidents, not instinct. Positively: (i) implements used as (ii) means to practical ends (iii) in the material world as (iv) expressions of intelligence.

**John Fernald** (2014, Fed economist) — dry/economic
> Ability to convert society’s resources (labor and capital) into output (valued goods and services).

**Ian Barbour** (1993, *Ethics in an Age of Technology*)
> Application of organized knowledge to practical tasks **by ordered systems of people and machines.**

Why this breadth is useful:
- “Organized knowledge” includes experience/invention, not only scientific theory.
- “Practical tasks” = goods **and** services.
- “People and machines” = institutions, not just hardware.
- Reminds us technologies differ a lot.

He also frames three views: tech as **Liberator**, as **Threat**, as **Instrument of Power**.

**Robert Friedel** (2007, *A Culture of Improvement*)
- Formal: **knowledge and instruments humans use to accomplish the purposes of life.**
- Excludes: purely mental or biological processes; purely descriptive/idea-level knowledge of the world; nature itself.
- Closing, informal: **a pursuit of power over nature.**

## Pattern cheat-sheet (how the definitions cluster)

| Cluster | Who | Core idea |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge → practical ends | Mesthene, Galbraith, Barbour, dictionaries | Organized/scientific knowledge applied to tasks |
| Means / purpose | Arthur #1, Thiel, Ferré, Friedel (open) | A way to do something humans want |
| Systems / assemblages | Arthur #2–3, Kaplan, Bain | Practices + components + social networks, not just gadgets |
| Production / efficiency | Mokyr, Mansfield, Fernald | Knowledge that turns inputs into more/better output |
| World-building / power | Sloan/Rhodes, Friedel (close), Hughes (*Genesis*) | Organizing/building a human world; power over nature |
| Attitude / method / totality | van Creveld, Ellul, Stiegler | Knowledge-as-stance, efficiency-as-society, life-by-other-means |
| Resource transformation | Andreessen | Lever on nature → something better |

**Shared tension Thierer keeps pointing at:** everyone uses the word; almost no one agrees on its edges (science vs tech, tools vs social systems, mental methods vs artifacts, progress vs mere change).
