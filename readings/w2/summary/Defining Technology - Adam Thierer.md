# Defining Technology — Adam Thierer

**What this is:** A growing list of how people define “technology,” kept as a resource.

**Thesis:** If you study or criticize technology, say how wide or narrow you mean the word — people use it to mean very different things.

- Many books on tech history and criticism never define “technology.” That is a problem.
- The list is not in date order. Most entries are modern; the word only became common recently.
- Dictionaries (Merriam-Webster, Oxford) treat it as applying knowledge to useful work, or as machines and engineering.
- Thierer’s favorite is Emmanuel Mesthene: technology is organizing knowledge to get practical results. John Kenneth Galbraith says almost the same.
- One cluster: a *way* to do what humans want — a method, a better trick, or intelligence made real. Examples: W. Brian Arthur, Peter Thiel, Frederick Ferré.
- Another cluster: more than gadgets — tools plus skills, practices, and social networks that also shape us. Examples: Read Bain, David Kaplan. Thomas Hughes adds that tech is messy and hard to define, like politics.
- Economists talk about production: knowledge that turns labor and money into more or better output (Joel Mokyr, Edwin Mansfield, John Fernald). Their wording is drier, and surprisingly few of them define the word at all.
- A bigger cluster: building a human-made world, gaining power over nature, or a whole attitude toward life (Sloan Foundation / Richard Rhodes, Robert Friedel, Martin van Creveld). Jacques Ellul uses “technique” (the push for total efficiency in every part of life), not just machines.
- Even experts cannot cleanly split science from technology (Rhodes). Fat, complicated definitions show how tangled modern tech has become.

## Lines worth remembering

- Say how broadly you mean “technology.”
- Mesthene (Thierer’s pick): organizing knowledge for practical purposes.
- The word can mean a tool, a method, a social system, or a way of life.
- Science and technology do not have a clean border.
- Economists often skip the definition too.
