Abstract

Against the backdrop of the increasing prevalence of non-military warfare, science and technology have become not only arenas for competition but also tools for coercive leverage where dependencies can be exploited. This chapter examines this increased securitization of science and technology through considering recent dynamics among the United States, the People’s Republic of China (PRC), and the Russian Federation. The chapter explores three primary mechanisms that states have tended to employ in this sphere. In a world of competitive innovation, states are pursuing proactive science, technology, and industrial policies to increase national capabilities in strategic domains of scientific research and critical technologies that can enhance competitiveness. For coercion or to maintain competitive advantage, states have become more deliberate about exploitation of chokepoints by leveraging particular resources, components, or technologies where one state has the capacity to exploit an advantage. And increasingly, the securitization of science and technology is transforming global innovation ecosystems, catalyzing a realignment of collaboration, in ways that are ultimately contributing to non-military warfare.
