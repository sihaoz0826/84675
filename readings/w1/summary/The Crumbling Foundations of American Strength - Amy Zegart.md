# The Crumbling Foundations of American Strength — Fast Read (all main points)

**Author:** Amy Zegart | **Foreign Affairs**, Sept/Oct 2024 (published Aug 20, 2024)
**Subtitle:** Knowledge Is Power—and the United States Is Losing It
**What this is:** A policy essay arguing that U.S. national power now depends on *knowledge power*, not tanks and territory, and that the U.S. is letting the foundations of that power rot.

**Core claim / thesis.** Future U.S. prosperity and security will depend less on blocking adversaries from getting U.S. tech and more on strengthening education, research, and the capacity to generate transformational technology at home. Knowledge is power — and it must be cultivated at home. Simply thwarting China will not produce the innovation the U.S. needs.

---

## Opening: power is not what it used to be

- 2022: U.S. intel was so sure Russia would roll over Ukraine that staff evacuated the Kyiv embassy.
- Traditional metrics said so: Russia 5th in world defense spending (2021); Ukraine 36th, behind Thailand and Belgium. War is still a standstill more than two years later.
- Ukraine’s showing comes from a highly educated population and a tech innovation ecosystem that produced drones and homemade weapons on the fly. Ukraine waged naval warfare without a navy: homemade drones destroyed nearly two dozen Russian ships and denied Russia control of the Black Sea.

## Tangible power vs. intangible power

**Old sources of power (governments could see, measure, control):**
- Conscriptable populations, conquerable territory, deployable navies, restrictable goods (oil).
- Spain 16th c.: armies, colonies, precious metals.
- UK 19th c.: strongest navy + Industrial Revolution.
- U.S. and USSR 20th c.: massive nuclear arsenals.

**New sources:** intangible resources — knowledge and technologies (AI) that super-charge growth, discovery, and military potential.

**Why governments cannot control them once “in the wild”:**
- Intangible; spread easily across sectors and countries.
- Cannot demand an adversary “return an algorithm” the way Bush demanded return of the Hainan spy plane (2001).
- Cannot ask a Chinese bioengineer to give back knowledge from U.S. postdoc work.
- Slogan: **Knowledge is the ultimate portable weapon.**

## Private sector now shapes geopolitics

Foreign policy has always been a two-level game (domestic actors + foreign adversaries). Now private-company decisions shape geopolitical outcomes, and U.S. private interests are not always aligned with national objectives.

Examples:
- Meta decides “what constitutes truth” for 3 billion users (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp).
- American CEOs with China business interests have met Xi about as often as Blinken.
- Elon Musk singlehandedly decided whether/where/when Ukraine could communicate via Starlink.

## U.S. government tools are deteriorating

- Appointments: at least a quarter of key foreign-policy posts vacant halfway through the first terms of the last three presidents.
- Debt: this year, first time ever, U.S. spends more on interest than on defense.
- Pentagon on stopgap budgets that fund existing programs, not new ones → no new R&D or weapons programs.
- Broken process hurts new/small/innovative firms. Big expensive systems persist; cheap new solutions wither.
- “If China were to design a budget process with the intent to stifle invention, send weapons costs through the roof, and weaken American defense, it would look like this.”
- K–12 and research universities — sources of long-term innovative potential — are in decline.

**Policy implication of the setup:** think newly about what U.S. power is, how to develop it, how to deploy it. Strengthen educational/research capacity and mobilize emerging tech for the national interest — don’t just deny tech to adversaries.

## Innovate and anticipate = “knowledge power”

Hard power (military) and soft power (values/attraction) still matter, but they no longer determine success the way they once did.

**Knowledge power** = advancing national interests by boosting the country’s capacity to generate transformational technology.

**Two essential elements:**
1. **Innovate** — produce and harness technological breakthroughs.
2. **Anticipate** — intelligence. Traditional spy-agency work (foreign intentions/capabilities) *plus* helping government understand implications of technologies developed *at home*, because domestic industry and foreign policy now blur.

Knowledge power’s primary function is inward, not outward. Traditional tools aim at foreign actors (threats, force, values). Knowledge power = marshal ideas, talent, and technology so the U.S. and partners thrive **no matter what China or any other adversary does**.

> Education and innovation are key to the United States’ ability to project power.

**How to measure knowledge power (hard to see/quantify):**
- **Educational proficiency.** Well-educated workforce drives long-term growth. East Asia nearly tied sub-Saharan Africa in GDP per capita in 1960; vaulted ahead over 30 years largely via educational improvements.
- **Geographic concentration of tech talent.** Physical proximity of top minds produces breakthroughs; labs and superstar teams beat isolation.
- **Health of research universities.** Innovation supply chain starts in campus labs, not companies. Firms must chase near-term commercial prospects. Universities do basic research: frontier questions that may take generations and may never commercialize.
- Without basic research: no radar, GPS, Internet.
- mRNA COVID vaccines looked overnight; actually 50+ years of university basic research (activate/block proteins; deliver mRNA to cells). Internet crypto from decades of pure math. ChatGPT / image recognition built on University of Toronto, Montreal, Stanford, etc.

## Brain drain: U.S. knowledge foundations are crumbling

K–12 in crisis; universities face global talent competition + chronic federal underinvestment in basic research.

**K–12 numbers:**
- 2023 NAEP: 13-year-old math and reading lowest in decades. Half of U.S. students fail state proficiency. Trends began *before* COVID.
- ACT: 6th straight year of decline. 70% of seniors miss college-readiness in math; 43% miss it in everything.
- PISA 2022 (15-year-olds): U.S. **34th in math**, behind Slovenia and Vietnam. Reading barely top 10; science barely top 20.
- More than a third below baseline math (cannot compare two routes or convert currency).
- Top end: 7% of U.S. teens at highest math level vs 12% Canada, 23% South Korea.
- Massachusetts (best U.S. state) would rank 16th in the world. Most states near global median. New Mexico ≈ Kazakhstan.

**Rise of the rest (redraws the knowledge-power map):**
- Since 1950, average years of schooling up; college graduates worldwide ×30.
- U.S. now depends on foreign talent. CS/EE doctorates to U.S. citizens/permanent residents: **78% in 1980 → 32% in 2022**.
- ~1 million international students/year; China largest share at 27%.

**Immigrant talent is an asset the U.S. is wasting:**
- ~45% of Fortune 500 (2020) founded by 1st- or 2nd-generation immigrants (Alphabet, SpaceX, NVIDIA).
- ~40% of U.S. science Nobel winners since 2000 are foreign-born.
- Outdated immigration: educate exceptional foreign students, then make many leave with what they learned. **Self-sabotaging talent system.**
- Supply chain works only if students still want to come and their governments allow it. Foreign universities improved. Share of Chinese students preferring Asia/Europe is rising. If Beijing restricted the flow, many U.S. labs and companies would be in serious trouble.

**University research lead is gone:**
- A decade ago U.S. produced by far the most highly cited papers. **Today China does.**
- 2022: China surpassed U.S. for first time on the Nature Index (82 premier science journals).

> The pull of the private sector is draining the sources of future innovation.

**Federal funding:**
- Only government can make large, long-term, risky basic-research bets.
- Federal research as share of GDP: **1.9% (1964 peak) → 0.7% (2020)**. China 1.3% of GDP on research (2017).
- 2022 CHIPS and Science Act was supposed to reverse this with billions for S&E research; those provisions later **scrapped** in budget talks.
- NIH: until 2014 majority of budget to basic university research; now more on clinical trials / applied research.
- NSF (premier nonmedical basic-research sponsor): CHIPS was supposed to **double** its budget this year; instead **cut 8%**. Grants smaller and shorter → scientists chase funds instead of doing research. Senior admin (anon): “standard NSF grants aren’t minimally viable.”
- U.S. still funds more basic research than China, but China’s research investment **+200% (2012–2021)** vs U.S. **+35%**. On trend, China overtakes U.S. basic-research spending within **ten years**.

**AI talent exodus academia → industry:**
- Fuels short-term commercial advances; drains basic research and the faculty who teach the next generation.
- One top CS dept: nearly 1/3 of senior AI faculty from a decade ago have left academia.
- Another: ~half of AI faculty part-time (anon estimate).
- Another lab: faculty/students can’t discuss research freely because some work at OpenAI under NDAs.
- Last year: **>70% of new U.S. AI Ph.D.s went straight to industry**, including a disproportionate share of the top students.
- U.S. AI commission: **“Talent follows talent.”**
- A generation from now policymakers will ask how they missed the talent crisis. They only need to look.

## A new power base (the playbook)

Assess, enhance, and use knowledge power.

**1. Tech intelligence / net assessment**
- Pentagon has legions comparing military capabilities. **No U.S. government office does the same for emerging technologies.** Must change.
- ODNI has started stronger ties with companies and universities; institutionalize them; share expertise faster.
- Congress: hold **annual technology net assessment hearings** with intel + academia + industry.
- Universities must share lab discoveries and implications. Example: Stanford Emerging Technology Review — accessible, regular briefings on 10 emerging techs (AI, bioengineering, space, materials, energy). Broaden/deepen: trusted expert networks; share with federal, state/local, and international partners.

**2. National compute infrastructure**
- Analogues: Eisenhower Interstate Highway System (1950s, growth + civil/military mobility); Ford Strategic Petroleum Reserve after 1973 oil crisis.
- Missing national-security infrastructure today: **computational power**. Almost every field now depends on AI, which depends on advanced compute.
- Training ChatGPT-3 would take **9,000 years** on a typical laptop.
- Only Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft can buy frontier chip clusters. Everyone else struggles.
- Princeton: dipped into endowment for 300 NVIDIA chips (≥$9 million). Meta: 350,000 of the same chips by year’s end (~$10 billion).
- Proposal: **national strategic computational reserve** — free/low-cost advanced computing via competitive grants (lease cloud or national-lab supercomputers); could also run smaller clusters. For researchers outside big tech and rich universities. Public-benefit AI, not just private profit. Helps keep top CS faculty in universities.
- Underway: NSF **National AI Research Resource (NAIRR)** pilot — 35 of 150+ proposals got compute/data. Bipartisan bill to make NAIRR permanent.

**3. Immigration + Pentagon acquisition**
- Let more of the world’s best students stay and work after U.S. graduation, with IP protection and espionage safeguards.
- SECDEF: make weapons-acquisition reform a top priority; fund affordability/innovation for real; tell Congress and the public that budget dysfunction makes the country less safe.

**4. Reverse basic-research underinvestment**
- Philanthropy cannot substitute. Schmidt Sciences AI2050: $125 million over 5 years. Drop in the bucket. U.S. government spends $125 million on **one F-35**.
- Bipartisan push (Sens. Heinrich, Rounds, Schumer, Young): fulfill original CHIPS promise; ramp nondefense AI R&D **tenfold to $32 billion**. Idea floating since 2021 — “a lifetime ago in AI.” Funding must increase **and** be delivered faster.

**5. Fix K–12 as a national-security priority**
- Warnings are old; treatment as urgent national security is not.
- 13,500 public districts: pay by years of experience + graduate degrees → physics teacher = PE teacher; best teacher = worst teacher.
- Pilots already working: Dallas, Houston, D.C. — incentive funds to evaluate and reward effective teachers; extra money if best teachers go to worst schools. Study and scale.

## Closing

None of this is easy. Without it, U.S. knowledge capacity keeps eroding and U.S. power weakens.

Washington has clung to export controls and outbound-investment limits to keep China from U.S. tech. **Simply thwarting China will do nothing to spur the long-term innovation the United States needs.**

> Now more than ever, Washington must understand that knowledge is power—and that it must be cultivated at home.
